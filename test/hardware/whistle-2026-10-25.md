## Attached-board installation and startup, 20:39–20:44 Sydney

Windows initially exposed the monitor's composite `303a:8360` device on bus
`3-1`; WSL attachment failed as busy even after force-binding. The owner used
the documented BOOT/RESET sequence, producing the ROM `303a:1001` identity.
That identity required its own administrator force-bind because UsbDk is
installed on the Windows host. Subsequent PlatformIO discovery found
`/dev/ttyACM0`, serial `B8:1F:3F:C3:9F:94`.

The selected `whistle` environment was rebuilt successfully before upload.
`pio run -e whistle -t upload --upload-port /dev/ttyACM0` succeeded in 147.96 s.
Esptool identified ESP32-S3 QFN56 revision v0.2, embedded 8 MB PSRAM and a 40 MHz
crystal. Bootloader, partition table, model and application writes each passed
hash verification. The model write was padded by one byte to 14,515,888 bytes;
the prepared file and its pinned checksum remain unchanged. The supported
watchdog reset completed. USB/IP detached during reset and was reattached.

[Upload log](whistle-2026-10-25/upload.log) and
[115200-baud PlatformIO startup capture](whistle-2026-10-25/serial-startup.log)
record the actual operations. The capture used an interactive pseudo-terminal
with DTR/RTS inactive. Its 40-second timeout ended the host monitor intentionally;
it was not a board failure.

Serial showed application version 0.1.0, IDF 5.5.5, 240 MHz CPU, 16 MB flash,
8 MB PSRAM at 80 MHz, successful PSRAM memory test and ILI9341 initialization.
Microphone and touch initialization completed on the path to `ready` (the app
would stop on either error). The depth-4 model loaded and cached a 2,164,536-byte
head. At ready: 205,407 internal bytes free, 5,363,580 PSRAM bytes free and a
5,242,880-byte largest PSRAM block. Eight heartbeats through 40.496 s reported
214,463 internal bytes free and the same PSRAM free/largest/minimum values.
No panic, watchdog warning or runtime error appeared in this short idle capture.
This establishes startup and idle operation, not completed board inference.

Uploaded application file: 317,920 bytes, SHA-256
`d6a8a78f32f07c5088f1c41490525970ecb071362746c98e8f1ac6f7f1d06b8c`.
One physical recording/transcription was subsequently verified below; broader
accuracy/latency, LCD appearance and sustained inference remain open checks. At this stage the monitor had not been restored; its app/model regions were
replaced by Whistle. The owner subsequently requested and received a full
monitor 0.8.7 restoration, recorded in [the restoration evidence](monitor-restored-after-whistle-2026-10-25.md). NVS was outside the written ranges, but its
saved settings have not been read back or tested after restoration.

## Physical English transcription and owner confirmation

While the PlatformIO monitor remained open, a physical touch started recording
at firmware time 11.008 s. Automatic processing began at 15.988 s, consistent
with the five-second cap. The app reported `audio_ms=4920` after removing the
configured head/tail transients, language `en`, 11 tokens and `elapsed_ms=14832`.
Its full serial result was:

> This is a test of offline speech recognition.

The owner answered **“A transcript appears”** to the requested on-board test.
This confirms one successful touch-initiated recording and on-screen result;
it does not distinguish a Stop tap from automatic five-second completion or
validate every touch/page control. See [the physical test serial capture](whistle-2026-10-25/serial-transcription.log).

Before encoder allocation, PSRAM was 5,236,600 bytes free with a 5,111,808-byte
largest block. After inference it returned to the same idle values as startup:
5,363,580 free and 5,242,880 largest. The observed PSRAM low-water mark was
2,819,660 bytes; internal free heap was 214,463 bytes before and after inference.
Subsequent heartbeats through 50.896 s retained those values. No panic, watchdog
warning or runtime error appeared during this captured inference. These are
one-run observations, not systematic accuracy, multilingual or endurance tests.

## Install effect and recovery

The new table preserves the monitor NVS range `0x9000`–`0xdfff`; this firmware
never initializes or writes NVS. It replaces OTA/application/model/filesystem
regions with one app at `0x10000` (2 MiB) and one model at `0x210000`
(`0xdf0000` bytes). PHY data is at `0xf000`; old OTA metadata is no longer
referenced. Existing on-flash files/apps/models may be overwritten. Returning
to the monitor requires its complete named PlatformIO upload and speech images.
The subsequent monitor restoration retained saved Wi-Fi and the enabled avatar
preference. Other settings have not been systematically checked; see the
restoration evidence.
